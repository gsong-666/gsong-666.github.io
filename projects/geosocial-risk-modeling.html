03 / Safe mobility · Geospatial AI

Geosocial Risk Modeling

Discovering geographic and social factors associated with railroad-crossing trespassing, then combining them with long-term monitoring data to identify potential hotspots.

  • Geospatial AI
  • Demographic Data
  • Risk Modeling
  • Industry Collaboration
RISK / MAP

Question

Which crossings are most vulnerable to trespassing?

Risk is shaped by more than the crossing itself. Nearby land use, demographics, human movement, and the surrounding path network can influence where incidents concentrate.

Approach

Connect geosocial context with observed behavior.

The framework integrates mapping, Census, behavioral, and long-term monitoring data to evaluate risk and identify candidate trespassing hotspots.

Data / 01

Multi-source evidence

01

Geographic

Crossing location, nearby land use, and paths connecting people to the crossing environment.

02

Demographic

Census-derived population and community characteristics surrounding each crossing.

03

Behavioral

Observed trespassing volume and movement patterns across monitored locations.

04

Long-term monitoring

Proprietary field data used to examine whether modeled relationships persist in real settings.

Workflow / 02

From contextual factors to hotspot candidates

  1. 01

    Assemble context

    Combine geospatial layers, demographic records, and behavioral observations at the crossing level.

  2. 02

    Model risk

    Identify influential factors and estimate the relative likelihood of trespassing activity.

  3. 03

    Validate in practice

    Compare predictions with long-term monitoring data and real-world crossing conditions.

Industry collaboration

I led collaboration with TRAINFO to analyze proprietary monitoring datasets and evaluate predictive models using real-world trespassing volume, land-use, and path information.
